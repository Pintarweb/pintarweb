## 1. Strategy & Positioning

### Core Principle

Outreach is primary. Content is opportunistic capture. When you're chasing a warm lead, don't stop to extract a content insight first — close the lead. The content loop runs off the leftovers of outreach work, not as a parallel obligation.

### Strategy Stack

```
Layer 1: Outreach Engine (build-first pitch-second — existing GTM, systematized)
    ↓ produces content for ↓
Layer 2: Content Engine (LinkedIn, YouTube, Facebook Groups, Resources)
    ↓ supported by ↓
Layer 3: Partnership Flywheel (aircond parts suppliers, contractor referrals)
```

### Time Budget: 7 hrs/week

| Activity | Hours |
|----------|-------|
| WhatsApp outreach (10-15 demos + follow-ups) | 3 |
| Content capture & posting (LinkedIn + Facebook Groups) | 2 |
| Partnership development (research, visit, follow-up) | 1.5 |
| Review & planning (weekly) | 0.5 |

### Positioning

Not "web design agency." Position as a **growth advisor for tradespeople.** The marketing message: "I help aircond contractors get more calls from Google." The website is the tool, not the pitch.

## 2. Content Engine System

### The Content Loop

```
Build a demo site / audit for a prospect
    ↓
Extract 1 insight from the audit
    ↓
Write a LinkedIn post (10 min, highest ROI)
    ↓
Share in 2 relevant Facebook Groups (5 min)
    ↓
If energy/ time left: record a Short from the same insight
```

LinkedIn text posts compound within professional networks and take 10 minutes. Video takes 30+ minutes. **LinkedIn is primary. Shorts are a bonus when you have the energy.**

### Weekly Content Target

| Day | Format | Topic Source | Time |
|-----|--------|-------------|------|
| Mon | LinkedIn post + screenshot | Insight from last week's audit | 10 min |
| Wed | LinkedIn post | Prospect objection you handled | 10 min |
| Fri | Optional: Short or LinkedIn | Something you learned this week | 10-30 min |

No scripts. Write as if explaining to a fellow contractor over WhatsApp. Record video as if talking to a friend — raw, one cut, no face required (screen recording only).

### YouTube Strategy (Bonus Tier)

- **Channel name:** Pintarweb
- **1 long-form video/month** — 5-min screen recording of 1 audit breakdown (laptop + voiceover only)
- **Short-form cadence:** When you have energy, record 1-2 Shorts from LinkedIn posts that got engagement
- **Important:** YouTube's algorithm does punish missing thumbnails for long-form. If you post long-form, make a simple thumbnail (screenshot + text overlay, 2 min in Canva). Shorts don't need thumbnails.
- **Description:** 3 sentences + link to "free audit for your business" landing page

### Where to Post

| Platform | Why | Cadence |
|----------|-----|---------|
| LinkedIn | Primary — professional network compounds | 2x/week (mandatory) |
| Facebook Groups | Where contractors actually hang out | Daily 10 min (comment + share) |
| YouTube Shorts | Bonus — when you have time | 0-1/week |
| TikTok / IG Reels | Repurpose from Shorts (same file) | Only if Shorts exist |

### Facebook Groups: Daily 10 Min

**Which groups to join:**
- Search: "contractor Selangor", "aircond Malaysia", "tukang rumah KL", "renovation group Malaysia"
- Look for groups with 5K+ members and active daily posts (not spam groups)
- Join 3-5 groups. No more — you can't sustain more than that.

**What to post:**
- Share insights from your audits (e.g., "Saya check 10 contractor aircond area Cheras — 8 takde website. Rugi besar.")
- Answer questions when someone asks about websites, Google, or leads
- Never post a demo link cold. Always share insight first, then offer: "Nak saya check website awak? Free."

**What NOT to do:**
- Groups ban self-promotion fast. Comment on 2 posts before posting your own.
- No "RM 149/month" pitches in comments. Only DM.
- No links in first post. Wait until someone asks.

### Automation: Audit → Content Draft

When you finish an audit, run this prompt in OpenCode:

> "From this audit data, generate:
> 1. A LinkedIn post (3 paragraphs with a suggested screenshot)
> 2. A Facebook Group share (2 sentences, conversational BM, no link)
> 3. Optional: a 15-second YouTube Short script (hook + insight + CTA)
> 
> Audit data: [paste audit findings or config]"

## 3. Partnership Flywheel

Build relationships that feed warm leads without building demos for cold prospects.

### 3A — Supplier List: How to Build It

**Tool:** Google Maps (free)

**Search queries:**
- "alat ganti aircond Cheras"
- "spare part aircond Selangor"
- "kedai aircond wholesale KL"
- "aircond accessories supplier Kajang"

**What to look for:**
- Shops with high review counts (20+) — high foot traffic
- Shops that also do installation (they know the pain)
- 3-5 shops within 15 min drive

**Target:** 10 suppliers saved to a list.

### 3B — The Visit: What to Say

**Day 0 (Prep, 20 min):**
- Pull up their Google Maps listing
- Note address, open hours, customer review patterns
- Quick check: do they have a website? If not — even better angle

**Day 1 (Prep, 10 min):**
- Print a one-pager: screenshot of Razif's live demo site + Google Maps ranking
- Keep it simple: A4 paper, 1 photo, 3 bullet points, your contact
- Physical artifact > phone screen for kedai owners over 40

**Day 1 (Visit, 45 min):**

Walk in, buy something small (RM 10-20 part). Don't pitch immediately.

```
You: "Bos, saya Yus. Saya buat website untuk contractor aircond
around sini. Saya sempat buat satu demo untuk contractor kat area ni —
nak tunjuk?"

[Show printed one-pager — Razif's site + Google Maps ranking]
[Or show site on phone — 30 seconds]

"Benda ni saya charge contractor RM 149 sebulan. Tapi saya boleh
bagi client referral fee dekat kedai awak — RM 50 untuk setiap
contractor yang signup lepas refer dari sini. Nak saya tinggalkan
kad? Senang-senang je."

[Leave simple card with: name + phone + QR to a demo site]

"Kalau ada customer tanya pasal website, just pass my number.
Saya urus."
```

### 3C — Follow-Up System

| Time | Action | Tool |
|------|--------|------|
| Day 1 | Visit + leave card | In-person |
| Day 3 | WhatsApp: "Bos, dah sempat tengok demo tu?" | WhatsApp |
| Week 2 | Call/message: "Ada contractor tanya?" | WhatsApp |
| Month 1 | Visit again: buy another small part, check in | In-person |
| Month 2 | If zero referrals — deprioritize, focus on new shops | — |

Log each supplier in a table:

```
| Shop Name | Area | Visit Date | Contact Person | Referrals | Notes |
```

### 3D — The Referral Ask: Highest-ROI Action in First 90 Days

The most underused channel. Every conversation — win or lose — should end with a referral ask.

**When a prospect says YES (subscriber signed):**
> "Tapi sebelum tu, you ada kawan contractor aircond atau renov? Maybe diorang pun boleh guna benda ni. Boleh saya call diorang, atau you nak bagitahu dulu?"

**When a prospect says NO:**
> "Takpe, faham. Eh, since you dalam field ni, you ada kawan contractor aircond maybe tengah cari pasal website? Saya boleh refer you — bagi RM 50 referral fee, dia signup terus."

**When a prospect doesn't reply after 2 follow-ups:**
> "Think you may not be ready yet. No worries. By the way, if you know any contractors who might need a website, send them my number. I'll take care of them."

**At every supplier visit:**
> "Contractor mana paling rajin datang sini?"

**Scripted into the outreach flow:**

```
Day 0: First outreach → demo
Day 3: Follow-up
Day 7: Referral ask (win or lose)
```

Warm referrals close at 2-3x the rate of cold outreach. This is your single highest-leverage action in the first 90 days.

### 3E — Monthly Partnership Cadence

| Week | Activity | Time |
|------|----------|------|
| 1 | Research & list 5 suppliers in target area | 30 min |
| 2 | Visit 2 suppliers (lunch trip) | 1.5 hrs |
| 3 | Follow up on visits, chase referrals from past prospects | 30 min |
| 4 | Review: who referred? Chase follow-ups. Deprioritize dead leads. | 30 min |

### 3F — Price Objection Script

> "Mahal la bro, RM 149 sebuah."

First, make the problem tangible:

> "Cuba Google 'aircond service [area awak]' — siapa muncul? Tu duit orang lain."

Then pitch:

```
"Faham. Tapi cuba tengok — satu job chemical wash RM 180-350.
Website ni kalau dapat 1 customer baru sebulan pun dah cover.
Banding dengan kad nama — website ni kerja 24 jam, tak pernah
cuti. Satu job dah cover sebulan."
```

If they still push back, don't discount. Offer to start with 1 month instead of 3 (RM 149, no advance). Lower the commitment, not the price.

### 3G — What Closing Looks Like (Operational Definition)

"Closed" is not a handshake or a "maybe." It's a confirmed payment.

```
Prospect says yes
    ↓
Send Billplz payment link via WhatsApp
    ↓
Payment confirmed (RM 447 for 3-month advance at Asas)
    ↓
Client added to Google Sheet Tab 1 as "subscriber"
    ↓
Website build begins (5 working days SLA)
```

Tracking demo-to-close is meaningless if you never define the finish line. **Closed = payment link sent via WhatsApp and confirmed paid.**

**Onboarding script (send immediately after payment confirmed):**

> "Terima kasih! Saya dah terima payment. Saya akan hantar website awak dalam 5 hari bekerja. Saya akan update awak bila siap untuk review. Ada soalan, WhatsApp je saya terus."

This kills the buyer's remorse window. One message.

### 3H — Dead Lead Reactivation (Warm Dormant List)

By Month 2 you'll have 30-40 people who saw a demo and went quiet. They're not dead — just not ready yet.

**The cadence:**

| Time | Action |
|------|--------|
| **Month 1** | 2 follow-ups → referral ask → park in "Warm Dormant" |
| **Month 2** | One "checking in" WhatsApp with a new angle (different demo, different insight) |
| **Month 3** | One last ping before archiving |

**Month 2 reactivation script:**
> "Eh, lama tak chat. I baru siap website untuk contractor area [same area]. Nak tengok? Maybe this one lagi relevant untuk bisnes awak."

**Month 3 archive ping:**
> "Hey, just clearing my list. If you ever need a website, I'm around. All the best."

Log these in Tab 1 with a status: `Warm Dormant`. Track the next reactivation date. One reactivation can close faster than a fresh cold outreach — they've already seen your work.

### 3I — "Kena Tipu oleh Facebook" Content Framework

This is the core narrative for Phase 1 LinkedIn/FB content. Stop talking about "data ownership" — contractors don't care about that. Talk about what they FEEL: their Facebook posts getting fewer likes, fewer inquiries, and no control over why.

**The 3-part narrative structure:**

> **Hook:**
> *"Abang pernah perasan tak — post abang kat Facebook dulu dapat 50-60 like, sekarang dapat 5-10? Bukan orang tak minat. Facebook yang sorok. Abang kena tipu."*

> **Middle (the reframe):**
> *"Facebook bukan kedai abang. Facebook macam kedai orang lain yang abang pinjam. Bila dia nak tukar kunci, abang tak boleh buat apa-apa. Website abang = kedai abang sendiri. Takde siapa boleh tutup."*

> **Close (fear + solution):**
> *"Tahun lepas, Facebook tukar algorithm. Post bisnes kecil drop 40-60%. Abang punya page pun sama. Bayangkan kalau semua customer abang ada dalam phone abang sendiri — bukan dalam Facebook yang boleh berubah bila-bila masa."*

**3 ready-to-use LinkedIn/FB post templates:**

**Post 1 — The Algorithm Drop:**
> "Saya check 10 contractor aircond area Cheras yang aktif kat Facebook.
>
> 8 dari 10 — post diorang dulu dapat 50+ like. Sekarang? 5-10 je.
>
> Bukan content diorang teruk. Bukan orang tak minat aircond.
>
> Facebook yang tukar algorithm. Post bisnes kecil yang drop paling teruk.
>
> Customer yang abang ada kat Facebook sekarang — bukan customer abang. Customer abang = orang yang search 'aircond service Cheras' kat Google.
>
> Website = kedai abang sendiri. Facebook = kedai orang yang abang pinjam."

**Post 2 — The Dead End:**
> "Customer search 'aircond service Kajang' kat Google.
>
> Dia nampak page abang. Dia klik.
>
> Takde website. Cuma nombor telefon.
>
> Dia try call. Line busy — abang tengah atas bumbung fix compressor.
>
> Dia scroll ke competitor. Competitor ada website, ada WhatsApp button.
>
> Customer tu bukan hilang sebab abang tak bagus. Dia hilang sebab takde tempat untuk dia landing.
>
> Facebook post abang tak boleh tolong dia. Google yang tentukan siapa muncul."

**Post 3 — The Ownership Reality:**
> "Abang ada 2,000 follower Instagram. Bagus.
>
> Tapi bila Instagram tukar algorithm esok — abang hilang semua tu.
>
> Nama customer, nombor telefon, alamat — semua dalam phone abang ke? Atau dalam Facebook/Instagram?
>
> Customer yang contact melalui website — nama, nombor, alamat semua abang punya. Bukan platform punya.
>
> Website = abang control. Social media = orang lain control."

**3-line WhatsApp pitch for data ownership (reframed):**
1. *"Abang punya Facebook page, orang boleh scroll past. Tapi website abang kat Google — orang yang search 'aircond service Cheras' tu memang NAK service aircond. Beza besar."*
2. *"Sekarang abang bergantung kat Facebook algorithm. Bila algorithm tukar, inquiry drop. Website = abang control, bukan Zuckerberg."*
3. *"Customer yang contact melalui website — nama, nombor, alamat semua abang punya. Bukan Facebook punya."*

**Content calendar suggestion:** 1 "Kena Tipu" post per week for the first month. Rotate between the 3 templates. Track which angle gets the most engagement.

## 4. Measurement & KPIs

### North Star: Go/No-Go Decision (Month 4)

| Metric | Threshold | Status |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| **Active subscribers** | ≥ 3 by Month 4 | ☐ |
| Demo-to-close rate | ≥ 30% | ☐ |
| Monthly churn | < 10% | ☐ |
| Build time per site | < 6 hours | ☐ |
| Service quality churn | 0 clients in Month 1-2 for service-related reasons | ☐ |

If 3 of 5 miss — pivot pricing, niche, or approach. This is the single most important line in this document.

**Note:** NPS surveys don't produce meaningful data at 5 clients. Skip NPS until Month 6+. Until then, track churn reasons qualitatively: did they leave because of price, quality, or just weren't a fit?

**Niche expansion trigger:** Second niche (plumbing, electrical, renovation) considered only after ≥ 3 active subscribers AND < 6 hr build time consistently for 4 weeks. Expanding too early is the fastest way to dilute outreach quality.

### Weekly Pulse (check every Monday, 10 min)

| Metric | Where From | Target |
|--------|-----------|--------|
| Outreach sent | WhatsApp counter | 10-15/week |
| Demos built | /clients/ folder count | 2-3/week |
| Replies received | WhatsApp | — |
| Content posted | LinkedIn | 2/week |

### Monthly Health (end of month, 15 min)

| Metric | Target | Why |
|--------|--------|-----|
| New leads | 3-5/mo | Pipeline health |
| Demo-to-close rate | ≥ 30% | Pitch quality |
| Active subscribers | → 3 by Month 4 | Go/no-go |
| Supplier visits | 2/mo | Partnership progress |
| Referrals received | 1+ by Month 3 | Flywheel starting |

### Dashboard Structure

1 Google Sheet with 3 tabs:

- **Tab 1: Weekly Log** — date, prospect name, niche, area, outreach sent, replied, demo built, closed

  Status legend: `Cold` | `Contacted` | `Demo Sent` | `Closed` | `Warm Dormant` | `Archived`
- **Tab 2: Content Log** — date, platform, topic, notes (don't track "leads from it" in Month 1 — attribution will be fuzzy. Focus on output consistency: did you post 2x/week?)
- **Tab 3: Partners** — shop name, area, contact person, visit date, follow-up, referrals

**Automation:** End of week, run in OpenCode:
> "From my outreach log this week, suggest 3 content ideas for next week: 1 LinkedIn post, 1 Facebook Group share, 1 optional YouTube Short."

## 4.5 — Minimum Viable Week (When Life Happens)

Not every week will be a 7-hour week. Define the floor so a skipped week doesn't kill momentum.

**Floor:** 5 outreaches + 1 LinkedIn post. That's it.

| Scenario | Response |
|----------|----------|
| **Busy week** (3 hrs available) | 5 outreaches + 1 LinkedIn post |
| **Normal week** (5-7 hrs) | Full plan |
| **Sick / Raya / emergency** | Zero — take the week off. No guilt. Resume next week. |
| **Two weeks skipped** | That's a signal. Ask: is marketing actually the blocker, or is something else wrong? |

This keeps the streak alive without burnout. A 5-outreach week is still a win.

## 6. Automation Recipes

### Recipe 1: Audit → 3 Content Pieces

Run after finishing an audit:

> "From this audit data, generate:
> 1. A LinkedIn post (3 paragraphs with a suggested screenshot)
> 2. A Facebook Group share (2 sentences, conversational BM, no link)
> 3. Optional: a 15-second YouTube Short script (hook + insight + CTA to free audit)
> 
> Audit: [paste audit scores or key findings]"

### Recipe 2: Weekly Retrospective → Next Week Plan

Run every Friday after logging your week:

> "From my outreach log this week, suggest 3 content ideas: 1 LinkedIn post, 1 Facebook Group share, 1 optional YouTube Short. Focus on high-interest angles from conversations that got replies. If no replies yet, generate ideas based on common objections from cold outreach."

### Recipe 3: Supplier Visit Prep (Lowest Priority — ~10 uses ever)

Run before visiting a supplier — but honestly, you'll use this maybe 10 times total. Set it up last.

> "I'm visiting an aircond parts supplier in [area]. Generate a 30-second pitch for the shop owner:
> - I build websites for aircond contractors
> - RM 149/month subscription
> - RM 50 referral fee for them
> - I just need them to pass my card
> Keep it conversational Malaysian BM."
